## 31. ENY_SNAP_TURTLE — Mud Snap-Turtle
Squat mud turtles lurk beneath duckweed where ditches empty slowly into marshy backwaters. They strike with startling speed suddenly, shearing reeds and fingers with equal indifference always. After millponds were drained for wartime grain, their stranded survivors crowded canals near market towns. Canal boys probe weed with poles hourly, flipping baskers onto banks for soup pots quickly. Ordinary washerwomen dread their lurking shapes more than eels, for bites fester painfully for weeks. In battle they lunge from shallows unexpectedly, locking onto boots with bulldog determination always. A thumb pressed behind jaw hinges forces release reliably, a trick every marsh child learns. Banked stones and cleared weed reduce ambushes locally, though protected marshes preserve breeding stocks.
Combat: 4/1/1/1, always_physical.
Appearance: Broad mud-brown snapper bulks wheel-sized, hooked beak overhanging a blunt armored head. Tail ridged like a saw, claws dig deeply, shell algae-coated for perfect camouflage. States: duckweed-hidden submerged ambush / lunging boot-locking bite assault / flipped shell-scraping stagger / hook-gutted still defeat. Variants (2M/2F readable via tail bulk, reason: snapper sexes differ in tail and plastron shape): weed-green juvenile floater / algae-caked elder snapper male / broad-shelled nesting female / saw-tailed canal drifter.

## 32. ENY_GIANT_SNAP_TURTLE — Giant Lake Snapper
Enormous lake snappers dominate forested lakeshores where fallen oaks create sheltered underwater caverns. They drag down swimming dogs yearly, drowning victims before tearing meals apart patiently underwater. After ferry tolls priced crossings beyond reach, their swum channels became feared shortcuts for smugglers. Smugglers lash boats with iron mesh underneath, sacrificing speed for protection against rising jaws. Ordinary fisherfolk sacrifice hooked nets readily rather than wrestle monsters that outweigh grown men. In battle they surge upward beneath skiffs violently, capsizing anglers into churning bloody water. Barbed pikes aimed at soft throats deter approaches effectively, though only veterans strike true. Bounty harpoons thin elders near villages seasonally, while deep caverns shelter breeding giants always.
Combat: 6/1/1/2, always_physical.
Appearance: Massive mossy snapper outweighs grown men easily, shell ridged mountainously and barnacled thickly. Head broad as a shield, beak capable of severing oars, eyes cold and patient. States: cavern-hidden lake ambush / surging skiff-capsizing drag assault / pike-deterred submerging stagger / harpooned overturned defeat. Variants (2M/2F readable via size, reason: giant snapper sexes differ clearly in bulk): barrel-sized subadult / mossy cavern elder male / deep-bellied nesting female / scarred mesh-chewing raider.

## 33. ENY_RIDGE_SALAMANDER — Ember Ridge Salamander
Small ember salamanders shelter beneath volcanic scree where warm cracks vent heat through cold nights. They lick mineral crusts nightly, storing salts that make their skin taste foul to birds. After sulfur collectors stripped gentler slopes bare, their spotted colonies retreated to higher ridges entirely. Collectors shake sacks twice daily carefully, releasing bright juveniles back onto heated rocks unharmed. Ordinary highland children trade sightings like treasures while leaving breeding stones strictly undisturbed always. In battle they exude milky skin secretions rapidly, sickening dogs that mouth them incautiously. Rinsed hands and averted mouths prevent sickness reliably during handling by curious travelers. Freezing rains and shrikes thin numbers yearly, though deep cracks preserve breeding adults reliably.
Combat: 2/1/2/1, always_physical.
Appearance: Hand-long ember salamander glows orange-spotted against soot-black skin vividly. Tail paddle-flattened, toes splayed for scree climbing, eyes lidded against gritty wind. States: scree-crack sheltering bask / exuding milky sickening secretion assault / rain-chilled torpid stagger / shrike-picked emptied defeat. Variants (sexes subtle, spot-forms only, reason: salamander sexes differ only by snout shape): bright juvenile basker / soot-black ridge elder / blunt-snout male-presenting / round-bellied female-presenting.

## 34. ENY_ASH_SALAMANDER — Grey Ash Salamander
Stocky ash salamanders burrow through pine duff where forested lakeshores meet cold highland streams. They ambush worms patiently after rain, gulping prey whole with sudden vacuum snaps. After tanneries fouled lower brooks with chemicals, their clean-water colonies withdrew upstream into hills. Brook wardens test water with caged juveniles daily, reading deaths as warnings of upstream poisoning. Ordinary charcoal burners shelter colonies in damp log piles, valuing slug-eating service around huts. In battle they lash tails unexpectedly, splattering irritating mucus across eyes and open mouths. Washed faces and closed helms prevent harm effectively during crossings through occupied duff. Trout and dried summers thin numbers locally, though spring seeps always restore populations quickly.
Combat: 3/1/1/1, always_physical.
Appearance: Stout slate-grey burrower feels cold and muscular, limbs stubby but powerfully clawed. Skin slick with defensive mucus, tail thick as a thumb, head shovel-blunt for digging. States: duff-burrowed worm ambush / lashing mucus-splattering tail assault / drought-burrowed estivating stagger / trout-gutted limp defeat. Variants (sexes subtle, duff-forms only, reason: ash salamander sexes invisible outside breeding season): slate juvenile burrower / pine-duff elder / shovel-headed male-presenting / swollen-bellied female-presenting.

## 35. ENY_ASH_SKINK — Quick Ash Skink
Glossy ash skinks dart across sunlit courtyard walls where flies gather thickest at midday heat. They freeze between sprints perfectly, vanishing against lichen that matches their mottled grey backs. After granary cats grew fat and lazy indoors, their wall patrols became welcome pest control downtown. Bakers crumble crumbs along ledges deliberately nightly, keeping resident hunters close to flour stores. Ordinary apprentices race to spot baskers first, learning patience from their utterly motionless waiting. In battle they streak across feet startlingly, tripping runners with unexpected underfoot wriggles. Swept courtyards and sealed grain deny flies reliably, dispersing hungry colonies without any killing. Kestrels and cold nights thin numbers constantly, though wall cracks shelter breeding pairs through winter.
Combat: 2/1/4/1, always_physical.
Appearance: Sleek lizard darts finger-long plus tail twice body length, scales glossy as glazed pottery. Legs blur with speed, tongue flicks constantly, tail detaches readily when seized. States: wall-basking motionless vigil / streaking underfoot-tripping dash assault / fly-starved dispersing stagger / kestrel-seized tail-dropped defeat. Variants (2M/2F readable via throat color, reason: skink sexes differ in breeding colors): mottled grey juvenile / lichen-backed elder male / copper-throated courting male / pale-bellied female.

## 36. ENY_SAND_SKINK — Pale Sand Skink
Pale sand skinks swim through loose dunes where sparse grasses anchor shifting surface layers. They dive beneath sand instantly when hawks pass, navigating buried darkness by scent alone. After goat herds cropped stabilizing grasses short, their exposed colonies suffered badly from circling predators. Herders rest pastures in rotation now seasonally, allowing grasses and skinks to recover together slowly. Ordinary dune children dig gently for sheddings, trading translucent skins to healers for salves. In battle they wriggle between toes rapidly, tickling feet and breaking shield-wall footing badly. Tightened sandals and stamped ground prevent infiltration effectively during crossings through occupied dunes. Shrikes and trampled nesting bowls reduce numbers locally, though fenced grasses restore colonies quickly.
Combat: 2/1/3/2, always_physical.
Appearance: Limbless-looking pale swimmer gleams pearly, tiny legs tucked uselessly against a serpentine body. Eyelids fused transparently, snout wedge-shaped for sand parting, scales smooth as glass. States: dune-swimming buried concealment / wriggling toe-tickling disruption assault / trampled exposed fleeing stagger / shrike-speared limp defeat. Variants (sexes subtle, dune-forms only, reason: sand skink sexes differ only by scale sheen): pearly juvenile swimmer / sun-bleached dune elder / glass-scaled male-presenting / deep-bodied female-presenting.
