# Human Families 01-05 — criminals / gangsters / thugs / rebels / mercenaries (25 entries, LOCKED, Phase 1, no JSON)
Rule: city-free (revised versions). Taxonomy Human. All magic 1.

## 1. ENY_CUTPURSE — Cutpurse
Cutpurses work market crowds of lowland trade towns where coin moves faster than watchful eyes. They are opportunists raised in lean years after the empire’s fall, when a full purse mattered more than honest trade. Most carry a short knife less for killing than for cutting strings and frightening victims still. Ordinary folk fear the slow bleed of lost wages more than wounds, and merchants beat them bloody when caught. Town watches punish them with whipping and branding rather than hanging, leaving scarred veterans back on the same streets. They run in loose pairs, one to jostle and one to lift, scattering once steel is drawn. A cornered cutpurse fights wild and low, aiming to wound a leg and escape alleys. Travelers sew coin inside sleeves and never chase a thief alone at dusk.
Combat: 2/1/4/1, always_physical.
Appearance: wiry hooded figure, patched cloak, palmed knife. States: sidle / lunging slash / doubled clutch / sprawled with coins. Variants: 2M youth/elder; 2F girl/shawl woman.

## 2. ENY_BURGLAR — Burglar
Burglars break timber houses and store-rooms of forest and highland towns where walls yield to a quiet pry-bar. They know shutters and patrol-change hours, lore passed cellmate to cellmate since garrisons thinned. They avoid faces, preferring empty rooms to drawn blades. Households dread morning discovery, for stolen tools take seasons to replace. Courts hang repeat house-breakers, so elders carry rope-burns from near arrests. In open fight they swing crowbar and hammer with workman strength but no drill, only when trapped. They surrender quickly when outnumbered. Valley farms keep geese and barred doors against them.
Combat: 3/1/4/1, always_physical.
Appearance: dark-wool laborer, pry-bar, sack, soot-smudged. States: crouch / overhead swing / stagger / face-down on sack. Variants: 2M climber/breaker; 2F lockpick/carrier.

## 3. ENY_SMUGGLER — Smuggler
Smugglers move untaxed salt and iron along delta channels and marsh causeways where checkpoints miss every reed. They fattened after the fracture made every border a toll. Boatmen and carters first, knife-fighters second, they know fords soldiers forget. Common folk both use and resent them, glad for cheap goods, angry when cheap grain undercuts harvests. Patrols seize boats more than lives, leaving debts that drive them back to water. Cornered they fight back-to-back with hooks and cudgels, tougher than cutpurses from wet cargo. They rarely murder travelers, for bodies draw soldiers. Their names change each port, no manifest bearing truth.
Combat: 3/1/3/3, always_physical.
Appearance: oilskin waterman, hook-pole, tar-stained. States: balanced / hooking pull / reeling slip / sunk kneeling. Variants: 2M boatsman/carter; 2F river girl/ferry widow.

## 4. ENY_COUNTERFEITER — Counterfeiter
Counterfeiters file and wash coins in back rooms of market towns, stretching scarce silver thinner with each successor mint. Their crime needs no blade until found, when penalty is the noose. Often failed smiths or clerks who learned weights in honest shops before imperial pay stopped. Merchants fear them worst, for bad coin poisons every later trade. Informers are paid well, so masters trust no apprentice long and move presses by night. In combat weakest of this kin, flailing hammer and tongs in panic. They keep hired muscle when rich, else bargain and beg before swinging. Villagers test coins with teeth because of them.
Combat: 2/1/5/2, always_physical.
Appearance: scorched-apron artisan, burnt fingers, nervous eyes. States: cringe guard / hammer-flail / face-cover / slumped over coins. Variants: 2M filer/master; 2F clerk/washer.

## 5. ENY_FENCE — Fence
Fences buy stolen goods in locked shops behind chandleries and pawn-stalls of trade and timber towns. They never lift purses yet make theft pay, so watches hate them most. Many began honest brokers when roads ran, then learned unasked questions earn silver. Common folk pawn honest goods beside stolen ones unknowing, ledgers kept ciphered. Courts fine them ruinously to seize hidden stock rather than hang. A fence fights like a cornered shopkeeper, cudgel in hand, shouting for the watch while breaking law. He pays blood-money on the spot over trading blows. Shop-boys with staves sometimes stand with him.
Combat: 3/1/4/3, always_physical.
Appearance: heavy-coat shopkeeper, rings, ledger pouch, cudgel. States: arms-spread / chop / rib-clutch / seated among wares. Variants: 2M youth/elder; 2F widow/clerk.

## 6. ENY_GANG_LOOKOUT — Gang Lookout
Lookouts are the eyes of street gangs in crowded market and dock quarters where alleys turn fast. Mostly youths paid in scraps, they whistle and scatter at the first helm. They know every rooftop gap and cellar door from childhood games turned to work. Folk ignore them as stray children until a robbery follows their signal. Watches rarely hang them, settling for beatings that harden them into full members. In a fight they are weakest of the gang, throwing stones and knives from distance before fleeing. Cornered they drop blades and beg, having seen what happens to those who resist. Their absence on a street warns travelers more than their presence.
Combat: 2/1/3/1, always_physical.
Appearance: ragged youth, sling and signal-whistle, barefoot quick. States: perched watch / stone-throw / cowering / bound seated. Variants: 2M street boy/roof runner; 2F market girl/alley sister.

## 7. ENY_GANG_ENFORCER — Gang Enforcer
Enforcers collect debts and hold corners for gang bosses who claim streets the successor guards cannot patrol. They are broad laborers and discharged soldiers turned to muscle after imperial pay dried. Shopkeepers fear their knock more than thieves, for they break fingers as ledgers. Courts jail them when caught but bosses pay fines quickly. In open combat they are the gang’s true fighters, swinging clubs and chains with brawler skill. They stand ground where cutpurses flee, guarding runners behind them. They spare those who pay promptly and maim those who stall as example. Travelers learn to walk wide around their claimed lanes at night.
Combat: 5/1/2/2, always_physical.
Appearance: heavy brawler, club and chain, scarred brow, sleeveless vest. States: squared stance / club-sweep / head-shake stagger / flat on back. Variants: 2M young muscle/pockmarked veteran; 2F hard brawler/older matron.

## 8. ENY_LOAN_SHARK — Loan Shark
Loan sharks lend seed-grain and shop-stakes at crushing rates in towns where honest lenders fear to tread. Often failed merchants themselves, they keep neat books of ruinous debts. Debtors fear harvest more than winter, for the shark’s men come with the threshing. Law allows the paper but not the beatings, a line sharks cross nightly. They fight poorly in person, hiding behind enforcers, striking only with dagger when trapped. Their power is the ledger, not the blade, and burning it frees whole streets. Folk whisper their names but sign anyway when children hunger. A dead shark’s debts pass to crueler kin.
Combat: 2/1/5/4, always_physical.
Appearance: sleek moneylender, ink-stained cuffs, dagger cane, heavy purse. States: raised-palm order / cane-jab / clutching coat / kneeling over torn ledger. Variants: 2M slick clerk/grey master; 2F sharp widow/young reckoner.

## 9. ENY_PIT_BOSS — Pit Boss
Pit bosses run illegal fighting cellars in ore and garrison towns where bored laborers bet wages on blood. They were brawlers themselves before age taught them to profit from younger fists. Crowds love and fear them, for they fix bouts and punish talkers. Authorities raid the pits but bribed guards warn ahead. In combat bosses remain dangerous, fighting with knuckle-irons and grappler craft. They duel travelers who mock the house, seeking to shame rather than kill. Their champions stand beside them in real trouble. Villagers blame the pits for every missing son.
Combat: 4/1/3/4, always_physical.
Appearance: barrel-chested veteran, irons on fists, betting slate at hip. States: rolling shoulders / hook-uppercut / spitting stagger / sprawled across table. Variants: 2M scarred youth/fat elder; 2F hard fighter/older keeper.

## 10. ENY_SMUGGLER_BOSS — Contraband Boss
Contraband bosses command whole smuggling crews along deltas and marsh roads, above common smugglers in coin and cruelty. They own boats and carts bought with untaxed profit, paying river folk to look away. Unlike lone smugglers they bribe patrols and hire blades, growing bold enough to tax others. Folk call them river kings in jest, then pay the jest-toll. Law wants them more than any thief, but witnesses vanish. In battle they fight with sword and purse, flanked by crew, retreating by boat when pressed. They murder informers as policy, leaving bodies at fords. Their cipher-books map every hidden landing.
Combat: 4/1/4/4, always_physical.
Appearance: fur-trimmed captain, short sword, boat-cloak, signet of debt. States: arms-folded command / sword-cut / reeling to rail / slumped over charts. Variants: 2M young captain/grey boss; 2F river queen/older quartermaster.

## 11. ENY_ROADSIDE_THUG — Roadside Thug (reuse)
Roadside thugs haunt thin stretches of trade road between farm belts and forest edges where patrols pass seldom. They are failed laborers and deserters who learned that a drawn knife earns faster than a hoe. Travelers fear their whistle from treelines more than wolves. Villages nearby pay them off with grain rather than blood. Law hangs them when caught but the roads are long. In combat straightforward and brutal, rushing with club and knife, no feint. They break at first organized resistance yet maul lone walkers. They share loot in rough equal parts and abandon wounded freely.
Combat: reuse ENY_ROADSIDE_THUG (3/1/2/2).
Appearance: ragged footpad, club, patched leathers, road-dust. States: roadside slouch / rushing chop / reeling / face-down in ditch. Variants: 2M young deserter/bearded ambusher; 2F hard marcher/older camp keeper.

## 12. ENY_TOLL_BRUTE — Toll Brute
Toll brutes seize bridges and fords, demanding coin from every cart that must cross. Bigger than cutpurses, they rely on the river doing half the threatening. Boatmen and carters hate them worst, for no detour exists. Petty lords sometimes license them, taking cuts. In battle they fight with staff and weight, shoving victims toward water. They hold ground at narrow crossings where numbers count less. They flee only when crossings are held against them. Their toll-marks scar posts for seasons.
Combat: 4/1/2/2, always_physical.
Appearance: hulking guard, quarterstaff, toll-box at hip, wet boots. States: arms-crossed block / staff-shove / staggered spin / toppled into mud. Variants: 2M young heavy/bearded keeper; 2F broad warden/older collector.

## 13. ENY_ALLEY_MUGGER — Alley Mugger
Alley muggers work poor quarters of trade towns where lanes narrow past shuttered shops. Quicker than toll brutes, crueler than cutpurses, they strike from doorways. Night walkers fear their paired rush, one to hold and one to strip. They know watch-bells and vanish before boots arrive. In combat they swarm lone victims with knives, scattering from real guards. They target drunks and strangers, sparing locals who might name them. Their cuts are shallow but many.

## 14. ENY_CARAVAN_RAIDER — Caravan Raider
Caravan raiders strike slow wagons on open valley and foothill roads where dust hides numbers. Better armed than roadside thugs, they ride scavenged ponies and carry bows. Drovers dread their dust-cloud at passes. They ransom whole trains rather than murder, knowing dead merchants draw armies. In battle they harass with arrows then rush stragglers, avoiding pitched fights. They camp in broken country beyond easy pursuit. Their leaders keep count of ransoms like merchants. Strong escorts and horn-signals turn them aside.
Combat: 4/1/3/3, always_physical.
Appearance: mounted raider, bow and saber, dust-cloak, stolen harness. States: mounted circle / diving saber-cut / unhorsed stagger / grounded with raised hands. Variants: 2M young rider/bearded chief; 2F hard rider/older wrangler.

## 15. ENY_DOOR_BOUNCER — Door Bouncer
Door bouncers guard gambling cellars and fences’ shops, hired fists at every illicit door. Chosen for size, they end trouble with throws rather than blades. Patrons respect their arms more than any law inside. They answer to pit bosses and gang enforcers. In combat they grapple and hurl, dangerous unarmed, slow with weapons. They hold doorways where footing is tight. They rarely chase beyond the step.
Combat: 5/1/2/2, always_physical. (Note: condensed sketch in chat; locked content identical.)
Appearance: massive doorman, bare arms, grappling belt, bruised knuckles. States: doorframe block / grapple-toss / shaken head / seated against door. Variants: 2M young ox/older scarred; 2F tall wrestler/matron guard.

## 16. ENY_BREAD_RIOTER — Bread Rioter
Bread rioters rise in hungry towns when grain prices outrun wages after bad harvests. They are bakers’ wives, laborers, and youths, not soldiers, armed with stones and staves. Folk pity and fear them together, for their cause is just and their fire spreads. Authorities answer with water and batons first, steel only if stores burn. In combat they swarm and shove, dangerous in crowds, helpless alone. They break quickly once leaders fall. Their barricades of carts vanish by morning. Travelers avoid market squares on riot days.
Combat: 2/1/2/2, always_physical.
Appearance: flour-dusted townsfolk, stave and stone, apron and shawl. States: shouting advance / stone-hurl / scattering stumble / seated with hands up. Variants: 2M young laborer/grey baker; 2F market wife/young daughter.

## 17. ENY_DESERTER_CAPTAIN — Deserter Captain
Deserter captains lead bands of runaway soldiers who refuse empty pay and endless marches. They keep drill and banners, living off forced levies in hill country. Villagers feed them from fear, remembering they were once protectors. Commanders hunt them as examples. In battle the most disciplined rebels, fighting in ordered ranks with spear and shield. They withdraw in good order rather than rout. They parley for pardons between raids. Their desertion spreads if pay stays thin.
Combat: 5/1/4/4, always_physical.
Appearance: weathered officer, dented cuirass, spear, faded sash. States: ordered line / spear-thrust / shield-brace stagger / kneeling with grounded spear. Variants: 2M young lieutenant/grey captain; 2F hard sergeant/older quartermaster.

## 18. ENY_HEDGE_PREACHER — Hedge Preacher
Hedge preachers gather crowds in fields and barns, blaming tolls and hunger on corrupt stewards. They carry no weapon but their tongue, yet their words arm others. Listeners bring food and hide them from patrols. Authorities arrest them for sedition, making martyrs. In combat they fight weakly with staff, shielded by believers. Their strength is leadership, holding mobs together under blows. Capture them and crowds thin. Their sermons promise the old empire’s justice without naming heirs.
Combat: 2/1/5/5, always_physical.
Appearance: travel-worn speaker, staff and satchel of tracts, hoarse voice. States: raised-arms address / staff-swing / bowed stagger / bound kneeling. Variants: 2M young speaker/bearded elder; 2F stern widow/young prophetess.

## 19. ENY_TOLL_BREAKER — Toll Breaker
Toll breakers smash toll-gates and burn tax posts along roads burdened with triple duties. They are carters and drovers who decided tribute costs more than rebellion. Traders secretly cheer while publicly condemning. Guards rebuild posts each time. In combat they wield sledge and axe against wood and men alike, strong from labor. They strike at dawn and melt into farms. They spare travelers and share seized coin. Their marks — broken beams — warn the next post.
Combat: 4/1/3/3, always_physical.
Appearance: burly carter, sledgehammer, toll-token necklace of victories. States: gate-facing stance / beam-smash / shoulder-wrench stagger / sprawled on broken gate. Variants: 2M young driver/broad elder; 2F hard teamster/older smith.

## 20. ENY_STUDENT_AGITATOR — Student Agitator
Student agitators are clerk-school youths who print broadsides against stewards and lead marches. Quick with words and pamphlets, slow with blades. Townsfolk smile at their fire until stones fly. Masters expel them, jails fill with them. In combat weakest rebels, waving knives and torches with more noise than skill. They rally others rather than win fights. Their ink-stained hands betray them after. Disperse the press and the march fades.

## 21. ENY_FREE_SPEAR — Free Spear
Free spears sell their shields season by season to whoever feeds, guarding caravans and work gangs. They are landless farm sons who learned drill from retired soldiers. Employers value their steady line over courage. Villagers hire them for harvest guards. In combat solid line infantry with spear and shield, neither fierce nor brittle. They hold while paid and march off when coin ends. They rarely hate their foes. Their contracts are sealed with hand-clasps, not oaths.

## 22. ENY_COIN_BOW — Coin Bow
Coin bows are hired archers from forest-edge hamlets who track game as well as men. They guard herds and ambush rivals’ convoys for pay. Their arrows feed employers before battles start. Folk hire them against wolves as gladly as against thieves. In combat they loose from cover then fall back with knives, avoiding melee. They quit lost causes early. Their bowstrings are their livelihood, oiled nightly.

## 23. ENY_CAMPFOLLOWER_BLADE — Camp Follower Blade
Camp blades are hangers-on turned fighters who cook, mend, and rob battlefields between hires. They know every army’s leavings. Soldiers mock but use them for dirty work. In combat scrappy with cleaver and cudgel, brave only in packs. They loot first and fight second. Their carts follow every column. Peace starves them into thievery.

## 24. ENY_SIEGE_HAND — Siege Hand
Siege hands are laborers skilled with ram, ladder, and mattock, hired for assaults on walled holds. Broad backs from quarry and mine work. Garrison towns dread their arrival more than knights. In combat slow but crushing with maul and shield, deadly at gates, clumsy in open. They dig and hammer under fire. Their pay runs higher for breach work. Without walls to break they hire as guards.

## 25. ENY_WAR_DOG_HANDLER — War Dog Handler
War dog handlers lead mastiff packs for patrols and debt-taking, hired where men are few. They raise pups on blood and whistle-commands. Villagers fear the dogs more than masters. Law holds masters liable for bites. In combat they send dogs first then strike with spear, fighting as pairs. If dogs fall they lose heart. Their leashes and whistles mark them.
